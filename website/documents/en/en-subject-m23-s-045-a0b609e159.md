| 10. | Objectives of the course programme (competences) | The goal of the course is for the student to become familiar with the ways H/FOSS projects (Humanitarian, Free and Open Source Software) operate and the opportunities for involvement, and to gain experience through active participation in an ongoing H/FOSS project.    Competencies the student is expected to acquire upon completion of the course:  To understand the advantages and differences offered by the various licensing models for free and open-source software.  To understand the communication methods and ways of working in H/FOSS projects.  To gain experience by participating in some of the activities of an existing H/FOSS project. |
| 11. | Course content | Topics covered in this course:  Introduction to H/FOSS (Humanitarian, Free and Open Source Software).  Introduction to copyright and the right of reproduction, licenses used in H/FOSS, comparison and analysis of their benefits and drawbacks.  Teaching the development of H/FOSS and H/FOSS in education.  Models of student learning through hands-on work in real projects.  Involving students in H/FOSS projects, methods, and ways of active engagement.  H/FOSS projects that enable and accept student involvement.  Process of proposing projects and topics for participation in existing projects, development of a conceptual idea, and an implementation plan.  Project implementation using H/FOSS  Project evaluation  Organization of teamwork with open participants, organization of teamwork entirely electronically and remotely  Organization and use of portals for sharing, controlling, and managing code in H/FOSS (Examples: OpenHub, Github, Gitlab)  Organization and use of systems for free collaborative documentation in H/FOSS (Examples: Wiki)  Organization and use of distributed systems for free, direct communication and free social networks (Examples: IRC, Matrix, ToX, Mastodon, Diaspora)  Organization and use of defect tracking systems in H/FOSS |
