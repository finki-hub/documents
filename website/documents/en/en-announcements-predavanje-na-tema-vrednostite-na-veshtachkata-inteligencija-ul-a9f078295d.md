# Предавање на тема „Вредностите на вештачката интелигенција: улогата на економската антропологија во креирањето иднина“ – ФИНКИ

On Friday, October 21, at 12:00 PM, Prof. Dr. Fabio Maticoli from the University of Melbourne will give a lecture on artificial intelligence and the possibility for ethnologists and anthropologists to engage with this phenomenon. The focus will be on a recent project he participated in to create digital assistants for airline pilots.

The lecture, in English, will be held outdoors at the Ethno Club in front of the lower building of the Faculty of Mathematics and Physics.

A more detailed synopsis follows. Dr. Fabioli's biography is here. &lt;https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/profile/802134-fabio-mattioli&gt;

“The Value(s) of AI: Economic Anthropology in the Making of the Future”

What is the value of AI systems? This lecture uses a critical co-design approach to describe the complex values associated with AI systems designed to support pilots (Digital Flight Assistants).

While airlines saw DFAs as ways to cut costs, pilots who participated in our research saw the digital flight assistants as teammates who would be generally untrustworthy, but, under the right circumstances, very helpful. This evaluation relied upon comparing the DFA with human co-pilots alongside three specific kinds of labor experiences where AI would fail to help them cope with increasingly degraded working environments. Yet, this mistrust was also couched in a layer of optimism. Paradoxically, in a future they saw as necessarily getting rid of (most of) the humans in the cockpit, DFAs were imagined as possible conversational partners, reminiscent of the golden age of aviation. Taken together, these two aspects suggest the need for an approach to the evaluation of AI that combines structural and emergent factors.
