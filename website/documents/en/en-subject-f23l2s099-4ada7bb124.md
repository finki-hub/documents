| 10. | Objectives of the course programme (competences) | The goal of the course is for students to become familiar with the delivery of services by government institutions to users: citizens, businesses, and organizations from the non-governmental sector, and the application of information technologies in these processes; to become familiar with advanced countries and advanced areas of e-government application through the analysis of examples, case studies, and best practices; to improve communication skills, both oral and written, teamwork in the development of a project assignment, and presenting the project assignment. |
| 11. | Course content | Lectures:  1. Introduction to e-government; Defining information systems and information technology; Defining the public sector and the application of information technology in the public sector  2. Theoretical models of governance: minimal state; corporate governance; new public management;  good governance; socio-cybernetic system; self-organizing networks  3. Phases of e-government and e-governance development  4. Benefits and risks of e-government and e-governance; Digital divide  5. Theories and models for e-governance: Chadwick and May's model of interaction between public administration and citizens from an information systems perspective; Layne and Lee's four-phase model; Watson and Mundy's strategic framework for e-democracy.  6. Flak and Rose's adaptation of Stakeholder Theory from management theory to the field of e-government or e-governance; The Henderson and Venkatraman model; adaptation of Henderson and Venkatraman's theory, as a dominant theory for applying information technology in the private sector to the public sector.  7. Review of global experiences in the most important application areas of e-Government and strategies for applying information technologies in the public sector by application area: e-Infrastructure, e-Business  8. Application of information technologies in the public sector by application areas: e-health, e-education, e-participation  9. Review of successful e-government strategies of the most advanced countries: Estonia, Slovenia, Austria  10. Review of successful e-government strategies of the most advanced countries: Denmark, Ireland, Finland, India, Singapore  11. Review of successful e-government strategies of the most advanced countries: European Union  12. Comparative analysis of the ICT projects of the governments of Macedonia, Estonia, and Slovenia  13. Determining the prospects for the application of information technology in the public sector and e-government in the Republic of Macedonia |
