| 10. | Objectives of the course programme (competences) | Upon completion of the course, the student is expected to understand the basics.  concepts from communication studies as a science, as well as to have in-depth knowledge in the application of existing techniques and  Insights for improving communication between the participants in communication. Special attention will be given to communication through new media and the way new media influence different social spheres. Students will be introduced to the basic principles of public opinion research and the use of new media (with a special focus on social networks) for building and promoting a brand (personal or corporate). |
| 11. | Course content | Lectures:  1. Introductory lecture; Introduction to the course objectives and content, the teaching method, and the type and amount of homework, essays, and project assignments.,  2. Fundamentals of communication theory; historical development of communications; communications and media; development and subfields of media (traditional, new, and ultra-new media); the impact of technological development on media and communications.,  3. Communication models, Three-level communication model, Distortions of the communication model due to media influences,  4. Storytelling, Story Structure, The Importance of Storytelling, The Influence of Media on Storytelling  5. Public Speaking, Public Speaking on behalf of an institution or company, Fundamentals of Public Relations  6. Social media as a medium, the networking effect, influence versus popularity,  7. Public Opinion Analysis: Needs, Principles, and Approaches; Techniques and Tools for Public Opinion Analysis; Using New Media as Tools for Public Opinion Analysis (Advantages and Disadvantages)  8. Brand and corporate identity and its connection to brand representation through the media, Using social media as tools for brand development,  9. Civic activism, media and social networks; Technological tools for increasing impact  10. Virtual communities, types and characteristics, virtual worlds, basics of cyberculture  11. Games as Media; From Games to Gamification; Application of Gamification  12. Phenomena arising from social networks, technophilia, technology addiction, technophobia    Practical Classes:  1. Establishing communication tools for the semester, defining criteria for well-written surveys and essays.  2. Practical exercises for illustrating various communication techniques  3. Practical exercises for illustrating communication models  4. Practical examples of applying storytelling  5. Analysis of public appearances, case studies  6. Practical exercises for illustrating the effects of social media, Case Studies  7. Analysis of case studies, defining hypotheses, defining the sample of respondents, application of tools for statistical analysis of the results  8. Case Studies  9. Case study analysis  10. Case Studies  11. Case Studies  12. Case study analysis (For example, fake news) |
| 12. | Learning methods | Lectures supported by slide presentations, interactive lectures, teamwork, case studies, guest lecturers, independent preparation and defense of a project assignment and a seminar paper, learning in an electronic environment (forums, consultations). |
